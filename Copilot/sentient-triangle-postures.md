# The Sentient Triangle — Postures & Experience Patterns

Source: Josh Clark & Veronika Kindred, *Sentient Design* (Rosenfeld Media). Adam's condensed working reference — see `triangle-diagram.png` for the visual map.

## The three pillar attributes

- **Grounded** (accurate and reliable): the system has the training and info it needs to deliver reliably accurate results.
- **Interoperable** (works across systems): the system can share data and instructions with other systems and formats.
- **Radically adaptive** (conceived and delivered in real time): the system morphs in real time to meet user intent and context.

## The edges (hybrid zones)

- **Collaborative** = radically adaptive + interoperable. The interface works flexibly with the user and/or other systems.
- **Autonomous** = interoperable + grounded. The interface is sometimes no interface at all — the system can confidently take action for you.
- **Iterative** = radically adaptive + grounded. The interface works back and forth with you to refine and improve results.

## The four archetypes and their postures

Each posture below has: **Experience Pattern** (what it is) · **Role** (form it takes) · **Embodiment** (how it shows up) · **Relationship** (how it relates to the user).

### Tools — grounded corner
*A transactional interface to perform specific tasks with precision and reliability.*

Corner-level provocations (apply to any Tools posture, especially Dedicated/Workflow/Inline tools which have no posture-specific ones):
- How does the tool connect to the steps before and after it?
- What contextual information can the tool access and use from the broader system?
- How can the tool smooth transitions between different stages of work?

| Posture | Experience Pattern | Role | Embodiment | Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dedicated tools | Single-purpose utilities | Stand-alone interface with simple controls | — | One-and-done operator |
| Workflow tools | Nodes in a multistep process | Piped to adjacent steps or systems | — | Reliable cog in a sequence |
| Inline tools | Contextual features within interfaces | Embedded controls | — | Enhancer at the point of action |
| Sculptors | Iterative refiner of generated content | Collaborative turn-based tool | — | Remote-control "hands" to shape a result |

### Chat — radically-adaptive corner
*A turn-based exchange of back-and-forth co-creation.*

Assistants have no posture-specific provocations — pull from Characters' "job to be done / capability signaling" questions below, since Assistants share that dialogue-driven, expectation-setting concern.

| Posture | Experience Pattern | Role | Embodiment | Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Characters | Entertainment or simulation | Dialogue-driven personas | — | Expressive role or scenario |
| Assistants | Task-focused helpers with light access to tools | Dialogue-driven mini-agents | — | Reactive support under user direction |
| Non-player characters (NPCs) | Participants in a common workspace | User-like presence in a shared interface | — | Teammates using shared tools |
| Bespoke UIs | Individualized interfaces | Shape-shifting interface conceived in real time | — | Mediators of adaptive experience |
| Intelligent Canvases | Platforms for open-ended experiences | Generative surface | — | Manifesters of user ideas and intent |

**Characters** provocations:
- Where could open-ended creative exploration spark insight (or maybe just some fun)?
- What scenarios call for an imaginative conversation partner who can play any role — entertainment, learning, or ideation?
- How might role-play and storytelling make an experience more engaging while helping users work through ideas or develop skills?
- What are the risks: how do you make clear the character is a performer, not an authority?
- When should this character be kind, when should it push back, and when should it bow out? How do you balance off-putting disagreement with dangerous flattery?
- How will you help users understand the character's character clearly?
- What's the conversation for? What's the job to be done? How will it make that job easier, clearer, more valuable?
- What capabilities are users most likely to assume? How can you signal limitations earlier without squashing engagement?
- What types of questions fall just outside the system's knowledge?

**Non-player characters (NPCs)** provocations:
- Which collaborative environments does your product support, and how could NPCs participate as natural users within them?
- What existing interaction patterns could NPCs leverage to avoid introducing new mental models?
- How will you ensure NPCs are helpful participants rather than intrusive interruptions?
- What signals will make the NPC's identity and activity transparent to human users?

**Bespoke UIs** provocations:
- How could targeted individualization elevate an otherwise static experience you're creating?
- What signals would inform that adaptability, and what would they tell you (and the system) about the user's goals and needs?
- What specific content or interactions would best meet those goals? How could they best supplement the existing base architecture?
- Should these additions be inline or off to the side? Loud or quiet?
- What are the fixed elements that ground the experience even as the interface morphs turn to turn?
- How can animation and other cues create a sense of presence or warmth — without the system pretending to be "too human"?
- What becomes possible when the system can discern sentiment or context beyond the literal command?
- How can you make accessibility an essential function of the bespoke UI — simplifying for cognitive disability, or adapting to a new medium (audio instead of visual) if that's what the user needs?

**Intelligent Canvases** provocations:
- When synthesizing through visualization, what formats formalize your users' thinking? How should the canvas consolidate raw content?
- What end-result artifacts are users trying to create, and how can the canvas accelerate creating them?
- How can the canvas help users move across seams in a process that otherwise feel like barriers?
- What becomes possible when static documents become dynamic, interactive artifacts? How could this make document creation easier for nonexperts?
- What are the fundamental capabilities of your application platform — what if you let users tap into them directly?
- What "purple crayon" notation or shorthand would support drawing new interfaces on this platform? What purpose-built interfaces might emerge?

### Agents — interoperable corner
*Emphasize cross-system capability and autonomous operation.*

Corner-level provocations (apply to any Agents posture, especially Conductors which has no posture-specific ones):
- When does delegation become abdication?
- What could go wrong?
- When are you asking too much of a system's capabilities?

| Posture | Experience Pattern | Role | Embodiment | Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Taskers | Specialist for executing complex but tightly scoped tasks | Headless or process-focused interface | — | Gofer subordinate that does one job really well |
| Alchemists | Transformer of formats and interaction modes | New channel or medium for data or content | — | Architect of alternative experiences |
| Stewards | Caretaker of a scoped, ongoing business function | System-level user | — | Teammate with independent responsibilities |
| Conductors | Strategic coordinator of complex, multisystem workflows | Dynamic command center for plans and progress | — | Executive delegator of high-level goals |

**Taskers** provocations:
- When is it helpful for the agent to reach beyond the screen?
- What tools are available to talk directly to the user or others, or to affect the physical environment via smart-home / IoT services? What are the social and physical risks of doing this?
- When is using a human-designed interface (phone, browser, email) the best or most efficient route for the task — and when is it counter-productive?

**Alchemists** provocations:
- Where is your content frozen? What if you could liberate it to a new experience channel?
- What new audience or use cases would that unlock?
- What becomes possible when content can fit a whole range of on-demand formats and channels?
- What alternative experiences of this source content would best serve the user — and what if they were fast and easy to deliver?

**Stewards** provocations:
- What does the user need to know to understand whether the steward is taking good care of its domain?
- What's the steward doing well, and where could it improve?
- What actions can help the steward do better, and how do you communicate that to the user?

### Copilots — center of the triangle
*Equal parts grounded reliability, interoperability, and radical adaptability.*

- **Ambient**: always available, tucked away when not needed. Summonable on demand, but also surfaces on its own when there's an opportunity to help.
- **Proactive**: listens and observes for opportunities to participate, jumping in at appropriate moments before being asked. Actions are offered only as suggestions.
- **Deferential**: proposes instead of imposes, seeking to support rather than direct the action.

**Copilots** provocations:
- What does conversation add to the task? Could inline tools or other patterns deliver the same value without pulling users out of their workflow?
- How can you embed intelligence closer to the activity or content you want to affect?
- What narrow moments of friction in your product experience could be eased by machine intelligence — what are your "hold music" moments?
- What sensors and data are available to detect opportunities and offer support? What context can only your system's environment uniquely gather?
- How can that knowledge support the user in the moment — and how might it inform actions beyond the screen?

## Notes for provocation-writing

- Postures near a shared edge (e.g., Sculptors and Bespoke UIs, both touching Iterative) will feel more similar to each other than postures on opposite sides of the triangle. For genuine bifurcation, favor postures that span different corners or edges.
- "Posture" is about *manner and relationship*, not just feature set — two postures can technically do the same task and still feel completely different to use. Always articulate the *relationship* column, not just the mechanics.
- The map is a thinking tool, not a catalog — it's fine (and often generative) to land "between" named postures or note where a concept doesn't fit cleanly.
